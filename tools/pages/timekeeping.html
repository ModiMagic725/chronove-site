The art of timekeeping

A watch is a counting machine. Here is how it counts.

The balance wheel

A balance wheel and its hairspring swing back and forth at a steady rate. Each swing releases one tooth of the escape wheel, and that is the tick you hear. Choose a beat rate to change the rhythm.

Shown at one-eighth speed so the motion can be followed.

Gear ratios

Everything between the barrel and the hands is arithmetic.

In a typical watch the minute hand turns once an hour and the hour hand once every twelve. A gear train with a 12-to-1 ratio between them makes that possible without any slippage.

The same arithmetic sets the beat rate. At 4 Hz the balance makes eight beats a second, and each beat advances the escape wheel by half a tooth. A fifteen-tooth wheel therefore turns once every 3.75 seconds.

Watchmakers choose gear ratios to balance three things at once: how long the watch runs on a wind, how smoothly the hand moves, and how much energy is lost to friction.
